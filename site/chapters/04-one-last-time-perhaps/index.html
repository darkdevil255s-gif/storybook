Chapter IV · A personal letter

One Last Time,
Perhaps.

Some feelings don't disappear.
They just learn to stay quiet.

A song that once belonged to us.

Kalle Kalle starts as the letter opens. Pause whenever you like.

Dear you,

I know it's been more than forty days since I last reached out to you.

Forty days of barely talking. Forty days of seeing each other occasionally, exchanging those little greetings that come with being neighbours, and then going back to our own lives.

Almost like two people who know each other, but don't quite know how to be around each other anymore.

I've got used to it, I guess.

Or at least, I've been trying to.

I know you don't feel entirely comfortable around me anymore. I've noticed it. I've felt it. And honestly, I've stopped trying to question it. Maybe keeping some distance is what makes things easier for you. Maybe it's what we both need. I don't know.

But sometimes, I do miss how things used to be.

It's been so long since we actually got some time alone together. Not those occasional conversations with everyone around, not those little interactions where we have to be conscious of who's watching, but actual time. Just you and me.

Around Diwali, we did get a little of that.

But something felt different. We were together, yet strangely quiet. Almost like those old days when we had so much to say but somehow couldn't say any of it.

Remember that night we walked?

And then I remembered that night.

Remember when we were walking together?

You told me something that I don't think I'll ever forget.

You said your brain wanted me to leave, pursue my dreams and make something of myself, but your heart wanted me to stay.

I don't know whether you remember saying that. Maybe it was just something that came out in the moment.

But I remember it.

And I think some words become special not because of how long they were spoken, but because of how deeply they were felt by the person listening.

My heart would probably cherish those words forever.

And you know what else?

I still smile thinking about that conversation about kissing you.

When I mentioned it, you immediately said no. And then, almost as if you were correcting yourself, you said, "It's my brain talking."

God, you have no idea how many butterflies that one little sentence gave me.

Even that moment in the Ray.

When I held your face and kissed your forehead, you burst out saying something along the lines of, "Yese pakadke bhi forehead pe hi kiss kiya?"

I remember the way you said it.

I don't know what exactly was going through your mind, and perhaps I shouldn't assume. But I remember how it made me feel. For a moment, I wondered whether there was something you wanted to say but couldn't.

And these are the little things I keep carrying with me.

Not because I want to keep questioning what they meant, but because they were moments when being around you felt so natural.

You said your heart wanted me to stay.

Kiddo, I genuinely love spending time with you.

I know things aren't the same anymore. I know there are people around us, families, circumstances, and a hundred other things that make even the smallest interaction complicated.

And I understand that.

I also know that I have an impact on your life, even if it's a very small one. Sometimes I wonder whether my presence makes you uncomfortable, whether you start thinking about what people might say, or whether you simply don't know how to behave around me anymore.

And honestly, that thought keeps me away more than anything else.

I don't want to become someone you have to avoid.

I don't want you to see me coming and start wondering how to get out of a conversation.

But sometimes I do wonder, until we eventually go our separate ways, couldn't we just stay in touch? Talk occasionally? Share a few laughs? Be happy around each other without making everything so complicated?

Unless, of course, there's someone or something else in your life that makes that difficult. And if there is, I would rather understand that than keep making assumptions.

Because I miss talking to you.

I miss the way you'd look at me when you wanted me to hold you, especially when you were feeling cold.

I miss those little expressions of yours that I somehow always managed to notice.

I miss being someone you could casually approach without thinking twice.

And sometimes, I miss us.

You know, there's a song, Husn.

There's a part that keeps coming back to me:

"Jab bhi padhe kahaani teri, lagta mujhe mere naam ka, zikr kahin bhi hoga nahi."

That thought scares me.

Not necessarily because you'll forget me. I know people move on, life gets busy, and memories eventually become quieter.

But what scares me is the possibility that someday you'll remember these days and wish they had never happened.

That I'll become a part of your life that you would rather not revisit.

I remember how things used to be.

I remember you crying for me. I remember you finding excuses to meet me, waiting for me, and being genuinely happy about those little opportunities to spend time together.

Even on my birthday this year.

I don't know whether anyone in your family knew about the chocolate you wanted to give me. Maybe you could have given it later, when everyone was around.

But you came.

You wanted to meet me and give it to me yourself.

And I remember that.

I know you care about me, kiddo. At least, I have seen that care in so many little ways over the years. Even now, when we've deliberately created distance between us, I don't think all of that suddenly disappears.

Remember when we were working on your portfolio?

The way you stayed beside me, holding my hand while we were doing our work. It was such a small thing, but it felt really good.

Then came the time when my abroad plans were getting closer.

Somehow, you started coming closer again.

Maybe because you thought I would be leaving soon. Maybe because you wanted to make the most of the time we had left.

I remember how you seemed to want those moments to last a little longer.

And when you found out I would be staying for Ganpati, the happiness on your face.

I miss that.

I miss seeing you genuinely happy just because there was a little more time.

And there's another thing I want to tell you.

I love how comfortable you've become with me over the years.

Remember when you used to hesitate before asking me to do something? Almost like you were wondering how to get me to agree.

Now you're perfectly capable of handing me your work, asking me to massage your feet, your head, your back, or making me hold your shoes.

And honestly, I love that side of you.

You can be as demanding as you want with me. I don't mind doing those little things for you. If I can make you comfortable, if I can make your day a little easier, I'd happily do it.

I'd probably spoil you rotten if you let me.

And yes, I still want that autograph on me.

I haven't forgotten.

I also miss you giving me work. Your documents, college-related things, random little tasks that somehow became excuses for us to spend time together.

Now you're a working woman. Independent, busy, managing your own things.

And I'm genuinely happy seeing that.

But if something ever comes up, you know I'm still here. You don't have to hesitate to ask.

And one more thing.

You promised me a hug before I left.

I know my plans got cancelled. I know things didn't happen the way we expected.

But I never got that hug either.

And somehow, that's one of those silly little unfinished things I still remember.

I never got that hug.

Our last proper conversation was about your internship.

I didn't get to know much about it. I was feeling a little low that day, and honestly, seeing you around everywhere while not being able to interact with you was getting to me.

So I asked you something.

"Would you rather see him happy with another girl or see him miserable with you?"

You chose to see him happy with another girl.

And I think that answer told me more than you probably realised.

Maybe you genuinely wanted me to be happy, even if that happiness eventually meant being with someone else. Maybe you had already made peace with the possibility of us never being together.

I don't know.

But I remember hearing your answer and feeling something sink inside me.

Because a part of me still wanted you to say the other thing. Not because I wanted us to be unhappy, but because some foolish part of me was still hoping that, somewhere, you wanted us too.

After that, I tried.

I really did.

I tried to get you out of my mind. I stopped reaching out. Stopped finding excuses. Stopped trying to create moments that might make either of us uncomfortable.

I needed some space.

Not because I stopped caring, but because I was beginning to lose myself in all the things I couldn't understand.

I wanted to be okay without constantly needing to see you, hear from you, or know what was happening in your life.

And for a while, I thought maybe I was getting there.

But then we spoke again. 22nd nov just a call for after dinner walk

Just a little.

And it was strange.

I wasn't suddenly expecting anything. I wasn't even looking for some grand moment.

Yet, after talking to you, I felt lighter.

I felt happy.

It reminded me of how good it feels to simply talk to you without carrying all those questions in my head.

And that's when I realised something.

I don't necessarily miss all the complicated things between us.

I miss the person.

I miss talking to you. I miss laughing with you. I miss those little moments when we could simply exist around each other without having to think about what they meant.

Forty days. And I still smiled when we spoke.

I don't know what the future holds.

I don't know whether we'll ever get back to those days, or whether this distance is something we'll eventually get used to.

I'm not writing this to ask you to choose me, to change anything, or to feel guilty about how things have turned out.

I just wanted you to know what these forty-plus days have felt like from my side.

I still care.

I still smile when I see you.

And yes, sometimes I still wish I could hold your hand, see that familiar smile, and spend a little more time with you.

Maybe that's all I really wanted.

A little more time.

A little less hesitation.

A few more conversations that don't have to end because someone might notice.

And perhaps, just one more hug before life takes us in different directions.

I don't know if this is the last time I'll ever write something like this.

Maybe it is.

Maybe tomorrow I'll see you, we'll exchange a smile, and I'll have a hundred more things I want to tell you.

But for now, I think I'll leave it here.

Take care of yourself, kiddo.

Keep smiling.

And whatever happens, I hope life gives you all the happiness you've always deserved.

I just hope that, somewhere in all those happy memories, there will be a little corner where you remember me too.

Not with sadness.

Not with regret.

Just with a smile.

Because that's how I would always want to remember you.
