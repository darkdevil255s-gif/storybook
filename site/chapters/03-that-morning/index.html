what he picked for her at the flower shop

He came to her town carrying more than just a plan.

On the surface, it was simple.
Meet her. Spend time. Talk.

But underneath that, there was something heavier sitting in his chest.

Questions he had buried for too long.
Feelings he could no longer carry alone.

He told himself that maybe, just maybe,
if the night stretched long enough…
if the world got quiet enough…
she would finally tell him the truth.

The first day slipped away.

She didn't come.

She had her reasons, and he didn't question them.

But somewhere inside her, something had already started tightening.

Because she knew.

He hadn't come all this way just casually.

And she had still let him wait.

So the next morning, she chose differently.

She woke up early.
Didn't overthink.
Didn't delay.

She just came.

He had already been waiting.

Leaning against the bike, pretending he wasn't checking the road every few seconds.

When she arrived, she came in a rush.

No sweater. No stole.
Just her… slightly breathless, slightly unprepared.

And for a moment, they just looked at each other.

Not smiling fully. Not speaking immediately.

Just… aware.

The ride

The Royal Enfield Bullet roared softly beneath them
as they moved through the quiet morning.

She sat behind him, careful at first.
Not too close.

Her hands resting lightly, almost hesitating.

"Where are we going?" she asked.

Again. And again.
Each time with a slightly different tone.

Curiosity…
then suspicion…
then something softer.

He didn't answer.

Because saying it would make it real.

The wind grew colder as they picked up speed.

And slowly, almost without thinking, she moved closer.

Her grip tightened.

Not out of fear.

Out of comfort.

He felt it.

Every small shift.
Every inch she came closer.

And it made his chest feel heavier in a way he couldn't explain.

They stopped at the flower shop.

Her name.
Right there.

He had noticed it long ago,
but bringing her there made it feel like a quiet confession.

He picked a sunflower.

Because he remembered.

She liked those.

Her

She didn't ask how he remembered.

She didn't need to.

She held the flower gently.

Running her fingers along the petals
like it meant something more than just a gesture.

Because it did.

Him

He noticed the way she held it.

And for a second, everything felt worth it.

They rode again.
Longer this time.

The silence between them wasn't empty anymore.

It was full of everything they weren't saying.

Then he said it.

"My room is nearby… I just need to pick something."

She didn't react immediately.

But inside, she understood.

Her

She knew this wasn't about picking something.

She knew he wanted time.
Space.
Something real.

Her heart didn't race out of fear.

It slowed.

Like she was stepping into something
she had already imagined.

They reached.

The door closed behind them.

And suddenly, the outside world felt very far away.

She started talking more.
Faster.
Random things.

Questions she didn't really need answers to.

That was her way.
When she was nervous,
she filled the silence before it could expose her.

He had imagined this moment so many times.

He thought he'd be calm.
Confident.
In control.

But now, standing in front of her…

He couldn't even find one sentence.

"Close your eyes," he said softly.

She did.

Without hesitation.

And then sat down on the sofa.

That wasn't how he imagined it.

And suddenly, everything he had prepared disappeared.

She opened her eyes again.
Looked at him.
Waiting.

He swallowed.

"This… this is where I wanted to bring you."

She nodded.

Then said quietly, "I should go."

That's when panic hit him.

If she left now, this would remain unfinished forever.

She stood.
Adjusted herself.
Turned slightly toward the door.

"Wait…"

His voice came out lower than he expected.

She stopped.

"Can I hug you?"

A pause.

A breath.

Then she stepped forward.

The Hug

It wasn't casual.

It wasn't light.

She wrapped her arms around him fully.

And he held her just as tightly.

For a moment, everything inside both of them settled.

The questions. The tension. The hesitation.

All of it… quiet.

Her

This felt familiar.

Too familiar.

Like something she had missed without admitting it.

She could hear his heartbeat.

Fast.
Unsteady.

And her own… wasn't much different.

Him

Her scent reached him.

Soft. Warm. Close.

Closer than he had ever allowed himself before.

And something shifted.

He moved slightly.
Not pulling away.
Just enough to look at her.

Then slowly… he leaned in.

A soft kiss on her cheek.

She didn't move.
Didn't stop him.
Didn't step back.

That silence between them changed.

He stayed close.
Closer than before.

His hand instinctively finding her hair,
brushing it back gently.

Her

That small touch…
that careful movement…

sent something through her
she wasn't ready to name.

She should have stepped back.

She knew that.

But she didn't.

Because part of her trusted him.

And part of her wanted to stay.

Him

He pulled her closer again.

Not forcefully.
Just enough that there was no space left.

Her hands shifted slightly against him.
Gripping. Then relaxing.
Then gripping again.

Neither of them spoke.

Because words would break something.

Time slowed.

There were moments where they almost stepped back.

Moments where reality tried to come in.

But each time…

they stayed.

Not going too far.
Not crossing into something irreversible.

Just enough to feel everything.

Eventually, it was her who broke the silence.

"We should stop."

He nodded.
Not immediately.
But he did.

And just like that, the moment began to fade.

They stepped apart.
Not suddenly.
Not completely.
But enough.

The air felt different now.

Warmer. Heavier.

They didn't talk much after that.

Because what had happened didn't need explanation.

The Ride Back

She sat closer this time.

Not hesitant anymore.

Her arms around him, resting more naturally.

Neither of them pointed it out.

But both of them felt it.

The road stretched ahead.
Wind brushing past.

The world moving like nothing had changed.

But something had.

Her

She didn't regret it.

That surprised her.

But she also knew…

this wasn't simple.

Him

He didn't feel relief.

He felt something deeper.

Like he had touched something real…

and now had to let it go again.

When he dropped her back, they didn't say much.

No promises.
No conclusions.

Just a quiet understanding.

Some moments aren't meant to be defined.

They exist.

They change you.

And then…

they stay with you.

Silently.

Completely.
